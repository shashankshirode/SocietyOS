1. Executive Summary

Society OS is a configurable society management platform designed for Indian residential communities. It supports small standalone buildings, cooperative housing societies, gated apartments, villa communities, mixed-use complexes and large townships through one flexible architecture. The product should behave like a modular operating system for a society rather than a fixed one-size-fits-all app.

The platform must reduce manual work for committee members, facility teams, guards and residents by digitizing visitor management, maintenance billing, complaints, notices, staff attendance, documents, NOC workflows, governance and resident communication. It must also preserve privacy, support audit-ready records and remain simple enough for senior citizens and guards to use daily.

Core product promise

Every daily society task should be quick, traceable and permission-safe. Small societies should see only simple modules, while premium communities can enable advanced workflows, hardware integrations and smart automation without changing the core codebase.

Promise

Meaning in the product

Easy to use

Residents, guards and committee members should complete frequent tasks in seconds, not minutes.

Configurable

Every major module should be switchable and society-specific through feature flags and settings.

India-first

UPI, WhatsApp fallback, cash/cheque, regional languages, tenant verification and society workflows must be native.

Privacy-first

Phone numbers, private chats, resident documents and child/senior data must be protected by consent and role checks.

Audit-ready

Documents, payments, NOCs, approvals, attendance corrections and admin actions must have immutable logs.

Future-proof

Biometric devices, RFID, ANPR, CCTV, smart meters and smart automation should be possible through an integration layer.

2. Product Vision and Principles

2.1 Mission

To simplify, digitize and improve daily life and operations for Indian residential communities through a configurable, easy-to-use and trustworthy platform.

2.2 Vision

To become the default operating system for Indian housing societies, from small buildings to premium townships, while remaining ready for smart automation, connected devices, marketplace services and enterprise operations.

Principle

Implementation meaning

Convenience first

The common actions like visitor approval, complaint creation, payment and notice reading should require minimal taps.

Configurable, not forced

A society should enable only the modules it actually needs.

Transparency by default

Payments, complaints, attendance, NOCs, approvals and committee decisions should be traceable.

Privacy by design

Sensitive data should be hidden by default and available only to authorized roles.

Low-connectivity ready

Guard and gate operations should support offline entry and later sync.

Modular engineering

Each module should have clear APIs, domain services, feature flags and audit events.

7. Deep Functional Specification by Module

Each module below includes business purpose, key capabilities and implementation guidance. The goal is to make the document directly useful for product owners, designers, developers and QA teams.

7.1 Society, Unit and Resident Foundation

This is the master-data backbone. All other modules such as bills, documents, visitors, complaints, parking, NOC and reports depend on clean society and unit records.

Key capabilities

Society hierarchy: society, phase, tower, block, floor, wing, flat, villa or shop.

Unit master with area, occupancy, unit type, parking and billing attributes.

Owner, tenant, family member and resident profile management.

Vehicle, pet, parking, rule acknowledgement and KYC status.

Bulk Excel import with preview, validation, duplicate detection and rollback.

Implementation guidance

Unit should be the central entity. Every important transaction should be linked to societyId and unitId so reports remain accurate and multi-tenant security is enforceable.

7.2 Visitor and Gate Management

This module replaces paper registers and fragmented gate apps with a fast guard workflow and resident-controlled visitor approval.

Key capabilities

Guest pre-approval with OTP/QR.

Delivery, cab, vendor, staff and material gate pass entries.

Gate-wise and guard-wise logs.

Blacklist/watchlist and security alerts.

Offline entry mode with later sync.

Implementation guidance

The guard app must work on low-end Android phones. The visitor entry path should require minimum taps and should gracefully handle network failure.

7.3 Maintenance Billing, Payments and Accounting

This module helps treasurers generate bills, collect payments, issue receipts and maintain audit-ready ledgers.

Key capabilities

Monthly maintenance bill generation.

Fixed charges, area-based charges, meter charges, parking charges and penalties.

UPI/payment gateway support with manual cash/cheque recording.

Receipts, flat-wise ledger, defaulter report and collection report.

Bank reconciliation and accounting export readiness.

Implementation guidance

Resident maintenance billing must remain separate from platform subscription billing. Do not delete financial records; use reversal or correction entries.

7.4 Complaints and Helpdesk

This module converts WhatsApp complaints into structured tickets with ownership, SLA and evidence.

Key capabilities

Complaint creation with category, priority, photo/video proof.

Assignment to facility manager, vendor or staff.

SLA tracking, escalation and reopen.

Resident feedback after closure.

Private/silent complaint option with misuse controls.

Implementation guidance

Every complaint should have status, SLA, assignee, audit trail and notification rules. Sensitive complaints must have restricted visibility.

7.5 Facility and Amenity Booking

This module manages common facilities such as clubhouse, gym, pool, sports courts, party hall and guest rooms.

Key capabilities

Slot availability and capacity rules.

Free/paid booking and refundable deposit.

Approval workflow and QR check-in.

Cancellation and refund rules.

Damage deposit and post-use inspection.

Implementation guidance

Avoid double booking using database-level checks. Booking status should control payment and refund behavior.

7.6 Parking and Vehicle Management

This module manages resident vehicles, parking allocation, visitor parking and wrong parking incidents.

Key capabilities

Vehicle registration and parking slot allocation.

Visitor parking pass and temporary vehicle pass.

Wrong parking and vehicle blocking workflow.

Violation history and repeat-offence escalation.

RFID/ANPR readiness for future automation.

Implementation guidance

Parking slot should be treated as an asset linked to unit and vehicle. Violation history must be visible only to authorized roles.

7.7 Resident Connect and Controlled Communication

This is a differentiating privacy-first messaging layer. It allows residents to communicate without exposing phone numbers or depending on admins.

Key capabilities

Resident-to-resident first-contact request with subject and reason.

Recipient can accept, reject, block or report.

Permanent chat thread after acceptance.

Department chats for accounts, security, facility and helpdesk.

Controlled group chats for tower, floor, owners, tenants or community groups.

Implementation guidance

Admin should not read private chats by default. Only reported messages and required metadata should become visible under a moderation workflow.

7.8 Inter-flat Issues and Dispute Mediation

This module handles sensitive neighbour problems through calm, trackable workflows instead of public arguments.

Key capabilities

Water leakage, noise, renovation disturbance, parking, pet nuisance and damage claims.

Evidence upload and affected-flat tagging.

Polite notification to involved flat.

Facility inspection and mediation assignment.

Resolution proposal, acceptance and closure proof.

Implementation guidance

Dispute cases should be separate from normal complaints because they involve multiple parties and stricter visibility rules.

7.9 Governance, AGM and Elections

This module helps societies run formal governance workflows in a transparent and auditable way.

Key capabilities

AGM/SGM notice, agenda, RSVP and attendance.

Proxy authorization and question submission.

Minutes of meeting and resolution tracker.

Owner-only polls and e-voting when enabled.

Election nomination, candidate profile and result publication.

Implementation guidance

E-voting should remain optional and disabled until society rules, eligibility and local compliance assumptions are configured.

7.10 Safety, Emergency and Senior Citizen Care

This module provides urgent support for residents, guards and families.

Key capabilities

SOS button, medical emergency, fire alert and lift stuck alert.

Family connect and emergency volunteer alerts.

Senior citizen simple mode, daily check-in and priority complaint.

Incident timeline and post-incident review.

Emergency notifications that bypass non-critical preferences.

Implementation guidance

Emergency flows must be fast, reliable and audited. Critical alerts should have acknowledgement and escalation.

7.11 Vendor, AMC, Asset and Inventory Management

This module helps facility teams manage common assets, vendors, contracts and preventive maintenance.

Key capabilities

Vendor database and contract tracking.

AMC expiry reminders for lift, fire, pumps, generators and other assets.

Asset register with location, warranty, documents and service schedule.

Inventory issue/return and purchase requests.

Vendor scorecard based on SLA, complaints and feedback.

Implementation guidance

Assets should store category, location, warranty, AMC, documents and service history. Vendor score should use objective operational data.

7.12 Marketplace and External Services

This module starts as a useful service directory and can later become a verified partner marketplace.

Key capabilities

Society-approved service listings.

Cleaning, car wash, pest control, laundry, appliances, pet care, elder care and tutors.

Packers/movers and interior designers for move-in/renovation.

Vendor ratings and request workflow.

Future verified partners and payments.

Implementation guidance

No intrusive ads during launch. Marketplace should feel like a community utility, not an advertising surface.

8. Owner, Tenant and Occupancy History Management

This is a high-value addition because societies often lose previous tenant and owner records in physical files, WhatsApp chats and email attachments. The system should maintain a clean historical timeline for every flat or unit, including previous residents, documents, NOC records, approval history and access deactivation.

Business purpose

For each flat, authorized admins should be able to answer: Who owns it now? Who owned it previously? Which tenants lived there? What documents were submitted? Was police verification completed? Was move-out NOC issued? Were dues cleared before exit?

9. Biometric Attendance Integration

The platform should support fingerprint or biometric attendance devices so the admin can see accurate attendance of security guards, housekeeping staff and other society workers. This is especially useful when security or housekeeping is outsourced and vendor bills need attendance verification.

Important privacy rule

The Society OS application should avoid storing raw fingerprint images or biometric templates. Ideally, fingerprint registration and matching remain inside the biometric device/vendor system. Society OS should receive only attendance logs such as employeeCode, deviceId, punchTime, punchType and sync status.

14. Security, Privacy and Audit Logging

The platform will contain sensitive resident data, tenant documents, payment records, visitor logs, staff verification documents and private communication metadata. Security cannot be treated as an afterthought.

Area

Required control

Tenant isolation

Every request must be scoped by societyId and validated server-side.

RBAC

Role and permission checks must be enforced for every sensitive action.

Document security

Use object storage with signed URLs or controlled download APIs; never expose public document paths.

Audit logs

Log sensitive actions: document view/download, NOC approval, attendance correction, billing lock, role change.

Privacy settings

Resident directory visibility and phone number exposure must be consent-driven and server-enforced.

Chat privacy

Private chats are not readable by admin unless reported or legally escalated.

Biometric privacy

Do not store raw fingerprint data in Society OS; sync only attendance logs.

Data retention

Visitor, staff and resident lifecycle data should have configurable retention policies.

Session security

Support device session management, forced logout and secure token expiry.

Financial integrity

Do not delete bills, receipts or ledgers; use correction/reversal entries.

17. Roadmap and Delivery Priorities

The roadmap below balances fast launch value with long-term differentiation. It keeps MVP practical while clearly reserving advanced hardware and automation for later phases.

Phase

Feature set

Outcome

MVP

Launch usable app for real societies without overwhelming UI.

Resident Connect, privacy directory, smart move-in/move-out, owner/tenant history, resident document archive, parcel handover, inter-flat issues.

Differentiate from basic society apps and solve daily resident problems.

Phase 2

Digital NOC, rules acknowledgement, parking, facility booking, vendor/AMC, assets, renovation, governance, advanced reports.

Strengthen operational maturity and admin control.

RFID, ANPR, boom barrier, CCTV, smart meters, EV charging, smart automation, e-voting, advanced hardware integrations.

Make platform enterprise and premium-society ready.

Recommended MVP discipline

Do not build every advanced feature in the first release. Build core society setup, visitor management, billing, complaints, documents, staff management and basic attendance first. Keep advanced modules behind feature flags so they can be developed without cluttering the launch UI.

18. Acceptance Criteria and Implementation Backlog

Acceptance criteria should be specific enough for QA and developers. Every major feature should include positive cases, negative cases, permission checks, audit logs and edge cases.

User story

Acceptance criteria

Admin can bulk import residents from Excel.

System validates duplicates, previews data, imports records, links units and logs the import job.

Visitor management

Resident can pre-approve a guest.

Guest receives QR/OTP, guard validates it, entry is logged and resident receives notification.

Billing

Treasurer can generate monthly bills.

Bills are generated correctly, published, paid and receipted with ledger update.

Complaints

Resident can raise complaint with photo.

Complaint gets ticket ID, SLA, category, assignment, notification and audit trail.

Admin can view past owners and tenants of a unit.

History includes occupancy dates, documents, NOC status and access is restricted/audited.

Tenant/owner cannot complete move-out without clearance.

System checks dues, complaints, parking, access cards and documents before NOC generation.

Document vault

Admin can upload and control sensitive documents.

Document has type, owner entity, visibility, expiry, version and access log.

19.2 Developer rules

Never hardcode visible pricing in user-facing app during free launch.

All modules must check feature flags on frontend and backend.

Every sensitive admin action must create an audit log.

Do not delete financial records; use reversal or correction entries.

Documents must have access control, expiry metadata and access logs.

Guard app must support low connectivity and low-end Android devices.

Keep SaaS subscription billing separate from resident maintenance payments.

Do not store raw fingerprint data in Society OS; sync only attendance punch logs.

Previous owner/tenant documents must never be visible to new residents by default.

Move-out closure must require NOC unless an authorized role overrides with reason.

19.3 Final recommendation

Society OS should launch as a clean, free and configurable society management platform while keeping its codebase enterprise-ready. The product should handle standard society needs such as visitors, billing, complaints and documents, but also solve deeper operational problems: privacy-first resident communication, owner/tenant lifecycle records, previous document access, mandatory move-out NOC, guard/worker attendance, hardware-ready integrations, governance and safety. These additions make the product more useful than a basic society app because they reduce manual files, WhatsApp dependency, attendance disputes and unclear move-out processes.
